3.1

Négations avancées · A2

Objective: Say not, no longer, never, nothing, nobody, not yet, nowhere, and no/not any with correct placement.

Real communication often needs a precise negative: what you do not do, never do, no longer do, have not done yet, or cannot find anywhere.

Why this matters

At A2, ne...pas is only the starting point. These patterns let you answer naturally in conversations about routines, plans, shopping, work, and appointments.

Explanation

Most negative expressions wrap around the conjugated verb: ne + verb + pas/plus/jamais/rien/personne. With a vowel, ne becomes n'. For rien and personne, the negative word can also become the subject: Rien ne..., Personne ne....

Quick reference / pattern

  • ne...pas = not: Je ne travaille pas le dimanche.
  • ne...plus = no longer / not anymore: Je ne vais plus là-bas.
  • ne...jamais = never: Je ne vais jamais là-bas.
  • ne...rien = nothing: Je ne fais rien ce soir.
  • ne...personne = nobody/no one: Je ne vois personne.
  • ne...pas encore = not yet: Je n'ai pas encore fini.
  • ne...nulle part = nowhere: Je ne trouve mon sac nulle part.
  • ne...aucun(e) = no / not any: Je n'ai aucun rendez-vous lundi.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Tu vas encore au club ? — Non, je n'y vais plus. Do you still go to the club? — No, I don't go there anymore.
  • Tu as déjà payé ? — Non, pas encore. Il reste vingt euros. Have you paid already? — No, not yet. There are twenty euros left.
  • Personne ne vient ce soir, mais rien ne presse. Nobody is coming tonight, but nothing is urgent.

Common mistake / warning

Je ne vois personne answers “Who do I see?” Nobody. Personne ne vient means “Nobody is coming.” The same logic applies to Je ne fais rien and Rien ne fonctionne. Also, plus is “no longer”; jamais is “never”.

Quick check

Choose the meaning: (1) Je ne sors plus le vendredi. (2) Je ne mange jamais à midi. (3) Je ne comprends rien. (4) Personne ne répond. (5) Elle n'est pas encore prête. Meanings: no longer / never / nothing / nobody / not yet.

Guided exercise

Transform: Je vais encore au marché. → Je... / J'ai déjà fini. → Je... / Quelqu'un arrive lundi. → Personne...

Real communication exercise

Send a short message to a friend: say one thing you no longer do, one thing you have not done yet today, and one thing you never do.

Your Turn

Write three true sentences about your week using plus, jamais, and pas encore. Add one A1 detail: a day, a time, a date, or a number.

3.2

Ne...que & réponses courtes · A2

Objective: Use ne...que to mean “only” and answer naturally with short negative replies.

In conversation, a short answer can sound more natural than a complete sentence: Personne. Rien. Jamais. Pas encore.

Why this matters

Ne...que looks negative, but it is not normal negation. It helps you talk about limits: only five euros, only water, only in the morning.

Explanation

Ne...pas means “not”. Ne...que means “only” (= seulement). Short answers are common when the question already gives the context; use a full sentence when the meaning could be unclear or when you need to sound formal.

Quick reference / pattern

  • Je n'ai que cinq euros. = I only have five euros.
  • Je ne bois que de l'eau. = I only drink water.
  • Il ne travaille que le matin. = He only works in the morning.
  • ne...pas = not: Je ne bois pas de café. / ne...que = only: Je ne bois que du thé.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Tu as de l'argent ? — Je n'ai que quinze euros. Do you have money? — I only have fifteen euros.
  • Qui vient ? — Personne. Who is coming? — Nobody.
  • Tu as fini le deuxième exercice ? — Pas encore. Have you finished the second exercise? — Not yet.

Common mistake / warning

Do not translate Je n'ai que cinq euros as “I don't have five euros.” It means you have five euros, but only five.

Quick check

Choose: Does Je ne prends que le deuxième bus mean “I don't take the second bus” or “I only take the second bus”?

Guided exercise

Answer briefly: Qui est absent ? / Qu'est-ce que tu achètes ? / Tu viens souvent ici ? / Tu as un stylo ? Use Personne, Rien, Jamais, Pas encore, Aucun, Aucune, Plus maintenant.

Real communication exercise

At a shop, explain that you only have 10,000 shillings, you only want water, and you do not need anything else.

Your Turn

Write a mini-dialogue with one ne...que sentence and two short answers.

3.3

Pronoms toniques · A2

Objective: Use moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles after prepositions, for emphasis, comparison, identification, and possession.

These pronouns make French conversation clearer: who with, who for, who owns it, and who you want to emphasise.

Why this matters

You need tonic pronouns after words like avec, pour, chez, and sans; subject pronouns like je and tu cannot go there.

Explanation

The full set is: moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles. Use them after prepositions, for emphasis at the start of a sentence, after c'est, after que in comparisons, and with être à for possession.

Quick reference / pattern

  • je → moi; tu → toi; il → lui; elle → elle; nous → nous; vous → vous; ils → eux; elles → elles
  • Preposition: Je viens avec toi, mais sans lui.
  • Emphasis: Moi, je préfère partir lundi.
  • Comparison: Elle est plus grande que moi.
  • Identification: C'est moi. Ce sont eux.
  • Possession: Ces livres sont à elle.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Tu viens chez nous samedi ? Are you coming to our place on Saturday?
  • Lui, il travaille à huit heures ; elle, elle commence à neuf heures. He starts at eight; she starts at nine.
  • Cette voiture est à moi, mais le vélo est à eux. This car is mine, but the bike is theirs.

Common mistake / warning

After a preposition, say avec moi, not avec je; pour elle, not pour elle est.

Quick check

Choose the tonic pronoun: avec (je) / chez (ils) / pour (elle) / sans (tu).

Guided exercise

Rewrite: Je vais au café avec Paul. → Je vais au café avec... / Cette place est à Sarah. → Cette place est à...

Real communication exercise

Plan an outing: say who comes with you, whose house you meet at, and who pays for the tickets.

Your Turn

Write four sentences: one after a preposition, one with emphasis, one comparison, and one possession.

3.4

Verbes réfléchis · A2

Objective: Describe daily routines with reflexive verbs and the correct reflexive pronouns.

French daily routines often use a small pronoun: je me lève, tu te douches, elle se couche.

Why this matters

You need reflexive verbs to talk about mornings, evenings, hygiene, rest, and fun—everyday A2 communication.

Explanation

A reflexive verb has a pronoun that matches the subject: je me, tu te, il/elle/on se, nous nous, vous vous, ils/elles se. The pronoun goes before the conjugated verb.

Quick reference / pattern

  • se lever: je me lève, tu te lèves, il se lève
  • se lever: nous nous levons, vous vous levez, ils se lèvent
  • Common verbs: se réveiller, se lever, se laver, se doucher, s'habiller, se brosser les dents, se raser, se maquiller, se coucher, s'endormir, se reposer, s'amuser.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Je me réveille à sept heures. I wake up at seven.
  • Le lundi, je me lève tôt. On Mondays, I get up early.
  • Je me couche à vingt-trois heures. I go to bed at eleven p.m.

Common mistake / warning

Do not force “myself” into every English translation. Je me douche means “I shower,” not usually “I shower myself.”

Quick check

Match the pronoun: je __ repose / tu __ habilles / nous __ amusons / elles __ couchent.

Guided exercise

Conjugate se coucher for all six subjects, then add a time to two forms.

Real communication exercise

Describe your morning on a work or school day using three reflexive verbs and one time expression.

Your Turn

Write your evening routine in four short sentences. Include a day of the week or a date.

3.5

Réfléchis : négation, questions & ordres · A2

Objective: Put reflexive verbs into negatives, questions, and commands.

The pronoun stays before the verb in most forms, but in positive commands it moves after the verb: Lève-toi !

Why this matters

You will hear and use reflexive commands in families, classrooms, clinics, sports, and travel: get up, sit down, hurry up, don't worry.

Explanation

For negatives, put ne before the reflexive pronoun and pas after the verb. For questions, use intonation or est-ce que; inversion exists and is useful to recognise. In affirmative imperatives, te becomes toi.

Quick reference / pattern

  • Statement/negative: Tu te lèves. → Tu ne te lèves pas.
  • Everyday question: Est-ce que tu te lèves tôt ?
  • Recognition: Te lèves-tu tôt ?
  • Affirmative imperative: Lève-toi ! Levez-vous ! Levons-nous !
  • Negative imperative: Ne te lève pas ! Ne vous levez pas ! Ne nous levons pas !

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Dépêche-toi, le cours commence à huit heures ! Hurry up, class starts at eight.
  • Ne vous inquiétez pas, le bus arrive dans dix minutes. Don't worry, the bus arrives in ten minutes.

Common mistake / warning

Say Lève-toi !, not Lève-te !. But in the negative, te returns: Ne te lève pas !

Quick check

Which is correct? (1) Ne toi couche pas. / Ne te couche pas. (2) Lève-toi. / Lève-te.

Guided exercise

Make commands: se lever (tu), se reposer (vous), ne pas s'endormir (vous), ne pas se maquiller maintenant (tu).

Real communication exercise

Write three helpful instructions for a friend before an early appointment: one positive command, one negative command, and one question.

Your Turn

Create a short morning reminder with at least two reflexive imperatives and a time.

3.6

Pronoms objets · A2

Objective: Replace direct and indirect objects with le/la/les, lui/leur, and simple pronoun combinations.

Pronouns keep conversation light: instead of repeating Marie, le livre, mes amis, say je la vois, je le lui donne.

Why this matters

Object pronouns are essential for natural short answers, planning, shopping, giving information, and talking about people.

Explanation

Ask Qui ? Quoi ? for direct objects: le, la, les. Ask À qui ? for indirect objects with people: lui, leur. Pronouns usually go before the conjugated verb.

Quick reference / pattern

  • Direct: Je vois Marie. → Je la vois. Question: Qui est-ce que je vois ?
  • Indirect: Je parle à Marie. → Je lui parle. Question: À qui est-ce que je parle ?
  • Simple order: me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur.
  • Combination: Je donne le livre à Marie. → Je le lui donne.
  • Combination: Je montre la photo à mes amis. → Je la leur montre.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Tu as la clé ? — Oui, je l'ai. Do you have the key? — Yes, I have it.
  • Je ne la vois pas aujourd'hui. I am not seeing her today.
  • Tu peux me le donner ? Can you give it to me?
  • Donne-le-moi, s'il te plaît. Give it to me, please.

Common mistake / warning

Le/la/les replace direct objects. Lui/leur replace à + person. Do not say je la parle for “I speak to her”; say je lui parle.

Quick check

Choose the pronoun: Je vois Paul → je __ vois. / Je téléphone à mes parents → je __ téléphone. / Je prends les billets → je __ prends.

Guided exercise

Transform: Je donne le cahier au professeur. / Nous montrons les photos à Amina. / Tu ne vois pas Sophie ? / Vous pouvez me donner l'adresse ?

Real communication exercise

At work, explain that you send the report to your manager, show the photo to your colleagues, and give the key to a friend—then replace the repeated nouns with pronouns.

Your Turn

Write one affirmative, one negative, one question, and one imperative using object pronouns.

3.7

Verbes irréguliers clés · A2

Objective: Use six common irregular verbs in the present: partir, venir, dire, lire, écrire, mettre.

These verbs are not just tables; they help you say where you are going, where you come from, what people say, read, write, and put.

Why this matters

They appear constantly in everyday conversations about transport, messages, school, work, and plans.

Explanation

Learn the patterns: partir has singular forms ending in -s, -s, -t; venir changes to vien-/ven-/viennent; dire has the special vous dites; lire/écrire share readable plural patterns; mettre doubles t in the plural.

Quick reference / pattern

  • partir: je pars, tu pars, il part, nous partons, vous partez, ils partent.
  • venir: je viens, tu viens, il vient, nous venons, vous venez, ils viennent.
  • dire: je dis, tu dis, il dit, nous disons, vous dites, ils disent.
  • lire: je lis, tu lis, il lit, nous lisons, vous lisez, ils lisent.
  • écrire: j'écris, tu écris, il écrit, nous écrivons, vous écrivez, ils écrivent.
  • mettre: je mets, tu mets, il met, nous mettons, vous mettez, ils mettent.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Je pars lundi et je viens en septembre. I leave Monday and I come in September.
  • Qu'est-ce que vous dites ? What are you saying?
  • Elle lit le message et elle écrit la réponse. She reads the message and writes the reply.
  • Tu mets le livre sur la table ? Are you putting the book on the table?

Common mistake / warning

Do not regularise vous dites as vous disez. Do not forget the double tt in nous mettons, vous mettez, ils mettent.

Quick check

Recognise the pattern: Which verb has vous dites? Which has ils viennent? Which has j'écris?

Guided exercise

Complete: Nous ___ à huit heures. (partir) / Ils ___ de Gulu. (venir) / Vous ___ la vérité ? (dire) / Elle ___ un e-mail. (écrire)

Real communication exercise

Explain your Monday schedule using three of the verbs and at least one time or date.

Your Turn

Ask a classmate two questions using two different verbs from the list.

3.8

Pouvoir, vouloir, devoir · A2

Objective: Express ability, permission, desire, and obligation with pouvoir/vouloir/devoir + infinitive.

With these three verbs, you can manage real life: ask for help, say what you want, explain what you must do, and decline politely.

Why this matters

A2 communication often means negotiating plans: what you can do, want to do, and have to do.

Explanation

Pouvoir = can / be able to / may. Vouloir = want. Devoir = must / have to. They are usually followed directly by an infinitive: Je peux venir, Je veux apprendre, Je dois travailler.

Quick reference / pattern

  • pouvoir: je peux, tu peux, il peut, nous pouvons, vous pouvez, ils peuvent.
  • vouloir: je veux, tu veux, il veut, nous voulons, vous voulez, ils veulent.
  • devoir: je dois, tu dois, il doit, nous devons, vous devez, ils doivent.
  • Negatives: Je ne peux pas venir. Je ne veux pas sortir. Je ne dois pas partir.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Je peux vous aider ? Can I help you?
  • Vous voulez un café ? Would you like a coffee?
  • Vous devez attendre dix minutes. You have to wait ten minutes.
  • Je voudrais venir, mais je dois travailler jusqu'à dix-huit heures. I would like to come, but I have to work until six p.m.

Common mistake / warning

Do not add à or de after these verbs at this level: say Je veux apprendre, not Je veux à apprendre.

Quick check

Choose the meaning: pouvoir / vouloir / devoir. Which one expresses ability? desire? obligation?

Guided exercise

Complete: Je ___ payer par carte ? / Nous ___ partir lundi. / Elle ___ apprendre le français. / Tu ne ___ pas attendre ?

Real communication exercise

Write a short dialogue at an office: ask for help, say what you want, and explain what you must do before 15 August.

Your Turn

Write three true sentences: one thing you can do today, one thing you want to do this month, and one thing you must do this week.

3.9

Poser des questions · A2

Objective: Ask clear questions using intonation, est-ce que, inversion recognition, and common question words.

Better questions create better conversations: people, things, places, time, reasons, methods, quantity, and choice.

Why this matters

You need questions for interviews, travel, shopping, work, schedules, and friendship. Spoken French often uses intonation and est-ce que; inversion is useful to recognise.

Explanation

There are three main ways to ask yes/no questions: Tu viens demain ? (intonation), Est-ce que tu viens demain ?, and Viens-tu demain ? (more formal). Then add question words when you need specific information.

Quick reference / pattern

  • Intonation: Tu viens demain ?
  • Est-ce que: Est-ce que tu viens demain ?
  • Inversion: Viens-tu demain ?
  • Question words: qui, que/quoi, où, quand, comment, pourquoi, combien, quel/quelle/quels/quelles.
  • Qu'est-ce que asks about a thing as object. Qu'est-ce qui asks what is the subject. Qui est-ce que asks whom. Qui est-ce qui asks who is the subject.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Quel jour sommes-nous ? Quelle date sommes-nous ? What day is it? What's the date?
  • À quelle heure commence le cours ? What time does class start?
  • Combien coûte ce livre ? How much does this book cost?
  • Quand est-ce que tu arrives ? When are you arriving?
  • Qu'est-ce qui se passe ? What's happening?

Common mistake / warning

Quel changes with the noun: quel jour, quelle date, quels livres, quelles places. Use quoi often after a preposition: Avec quoi ? Pour quoi ?

Quick check

Pick the question word: person / place / time / reason / method / price / choice.

Guided exercise

Create questions for these answers: À huit heures. / Vingt euros. / Le quinze août. / Parce que je travaille. / Avec Amina.

Real communication exercise

Conversation interview: ask a classmate six questions about age, schedule, work/study, plans, price/budget, and a preferred day or month.

Your Turn

Write a natural mini-interview with at least one intonation question, one est-ce que question, and one question with quel/quelle.

3.10

Adverbes & mots indéfinis · A2

Objective: Add frequency, manner, and indefinite words to describe people, things, routines, and quantities.

A sentence becomes more useful when you can say how, how often, everyone, someone, something, a few, none, or nothing.

Why this matters

These words help you produce short natural responses and richer descriptions without moving into advanced grammar.

Explanation

Many adverbs go after the conjugated verb: Il travaille bien, Elle parle lentement, Nous sortons souvent. Indefinite words name non-specific people or things: someone, something, each one, none, nobody, nothing.

Quick reference / pattern

  • Manner: bien, mal, vite, lentement. Frequency: souvent, toujours, parfois, rarement, jamais.
  • tout/toute/tous/toutes = all/every; agrees with the noun: tous les jours, toute la semaine.
  • quelqu'un = someone; quelque chose = something; quelques = a few/some + plural noun.
  • chacun/chacune = each one; aucun/aucune = no/none + noun; personne = nobody/person; rien = nothing.

Natural French examples + English meaning

  • Elle travaille bien, mais elle parle parfois trop vite. She works well, but she sometimes speaks too fast.
  • Nous avons quelques rendez-vous mardi. We have a few appointments on Tuesday.
  • Tout le monde arrive en septembre. Everyone arrives in September.
  • Je cherche quelqu'un, mais je ne vois personne. I'm looking for someone, but I don't see anyone.
  • Aucune place n'est libre et rien ne change. No seat is free and nothing changes.

Common mistake / warning

Personne can mean “person” in affirmative contexts (une personne) but “nobody” in negative structures. Jamais is also a negative word when used with ne: Je ne viens jamais.

Quick check

Choose: quelqu'un / quelque chose / quelques / chacun / aucun / personne / rien. “a few books”, “someone at the door”, “nothing to do”, “no ticket”, “each one has a pen”.

Guided exercise

Improve these sentences by adding an adverb or indefinite word: Il répond. / Elle arrive. / Nous avons livres. / Je ne vois pas.

Real communication exercise

Describe a busy day: say how often you do two activities, mention a few appointments, and say one thing nobody does or nothing changes.

Your Turn

Write a final Track 3 paragraph: 5–6 sentences about your routine, plans, questions, and limits. Use one negative, one pronoun, one irregular verb, one question, and one adverb.
